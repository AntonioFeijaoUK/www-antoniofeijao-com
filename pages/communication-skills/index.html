    Communication skills. 10 ways to have a better conversation.

    intro¶

    Mastery of dialogue underpins every successful negotiation, team decision, and client engagement.

    The following ten principles form a compact operating code for high‑stakes conversation: they cultivate trust, surface hidden interests, and accelerate mutually valuable outcomes.

    Adopt them consciously and you convert speaking time from mere exchange of words into strategic leverage.

    Summary take‑aways¶

    Presence – undivided attention curbs misinterpretation and signals respect.

    Humility – avoid dogma; frame views as hypotheses, not verdicts.

    Curiosity – open questions unlock drivers, constraints, and creative options.

    Conciseness – say it once, stay on the main issues, keep momentum.

    Perspective – recognise that your experience is unique, not universal.

    Flexibility – follow conversational currents, adapt offers in real time.

    Integrity – declare knowledge gaps promptly and commit to verification.

    Active listening – reflect, paraphrase, and detect underlying concerns early.

    Empathy – brevity and respect for time demonstrate regard for others’ goals.

    Strategic focus – reserve energy for decisions that materially affect value.

    Applied together, these guidelines transform dialogue from transactional to transformational, turning every interaction into an opportunity for insight and durable agreement.

    ted video by Celeste Headlee¶

    10 ways to have a better conversation. Communication skills.

    source https://www.ted.com/talks/celeste_headlee_10_ways_to_have_a_better_conversation

    • 10 ways to have a better conversation
    • Celeste Headlee
    • TEDxCreativeCoast
    • May 2015

    1 - Do not multitask¶

    Core principle – give undivided attention

    • Focus exclusively on the speaker; silence devices, close the laptop, and maintain eye contact.
    • Example: In a contract‑review meeting, place your phone face‑down and take handwritten notes to avoid on‑screen distractions.

    Negotiation value – signals respect, reduces mis‑reads

    • Full attention diminishes misinterpretation and builds immediate rapport.
    • Example: The other party feels heard, so positional rigidity softens and options widen.

    2 - Do not pontificate¶

    Core principle – avoid preaching or certitude

    • Offer ideas as viewpoints, not edicts; remain open to being corrected.
    • Example: Say “From my data, X appears likely; what is your perspective?” rather than “Obviously X is true.”

    Negotiation value – fosters collaboration

    • A non‑dogmatic tone invites joint problem‑solving and creative trades.
    • Example: After you frame your proposal as tentative, the counterpart volunteers additional concessions.

    3 - Ask open‑ended questions¶

    Core principle – explore with “what, how, why” - even better if you use "Tell me..", "Can you describe...", "How do you fell..."

    • Draw out narratives and motivations instead of yes/no answers.
    • Example: “What would make this timeline workable for you?”

    Negotiation value – surfaces hidden interests

    • Broad questions uncover constraints, enabling tailored packages.
    • Example: You learn shipping speed, not price, is the real priority, allowing a profitable fast‑track option.

    4 - Do not repeat yourself¶

    Core principle – trust that a point made once is heard

    • Repetition signals insecurity and drains attention.
    • Example: State delivery terms clearly, then move on rather than re‑stating them each round.

    Negotiation value – maintains engagement

    • Fresh content keeps momentum and shows respect for time.
    • Example: Because you avoid loops, the session ends early enough for an extra agenda item that seals the deal.

    5 - Stay out of the weeds¶

    Core principle – focus on high‑value issues

    • Skip peripheral detail unless it alters outcomes.
    • Example: Summarise the warranty clause rather than reading it verbatim.

    Negotiation value – preserves energy for critical decisions

    • Parties stay strategic instead of mired in minutiae.
    • Example: Clarity on the main price‑risk trade‑off emerges sooner, accelerating agreement.

    6 - Do not equate your experience with theirs¶

    Core principle – avoid “I know exactly how you feel”

    • Acknowledge differences and invite elaboration.
    • Example: “Tell me more about how this affects your regional team.”

    Negotiation value – demonstrates empathy without appropriation

    • Genuine curiosity deepens trust and reveals nuance.
    • Example: The client shares localisation hurdles you can solve for mutual gain.

    7 - Go with the flow¶

    Core principle – follow conversational currents

    • Let ideas emerge organically; park side thoughts for later.
    • Example: Jot a note instead of interrupting when a tangential risk crosses your mind.

    Negotiation value – builds agility

    • Flexibility helps you pivot offers as new data appears.
    • Example: A spontaneous discussion of volume discounts leads to a tiered‑pricing breakthrough.

    8 - If you do not know, say so¶

    Core principle – admit uncertainty promptly

    • Promise to verify rather than speculate.
    • Example: “I shall confirm compliance costs and revert by 16:00.”

    Negotiation value – maintains credibility

    • Honesty prevents future concessions caused by misstatements.
    • Example: Accurate follow‑up figures avoid an embarrassing retraction that would erode leverage.

    9 - Listen¶

    Core principle – practise active, reflective listening

    • Paraphrase and check understanding before responding.
    • Example: “So meeting the Easter launch is critical; have I captured that correctly?”

    Negotiation value – uncovers objections early

    • Demonstrated listening lowers defences and reveals deal‑breakers.
    • Example: You detect unease about support levels and proactively sweeten the service package.

    10 - Be brief¶

    Core principle – pursue clarity and economy

    • Make points succinctly; prefer precision to prolixity.
    • Example: Use a 90‑second summary of benefits instead of a ten‑minute exposition.

    Negotiation value – respects attention span, keeps pace

    • Brevity sustains focus and creates space for reciprocal input.
    • Example: Concise statements enable rapid back‑and‑forth, leading to quicker consensus.
