## Implications

The December 2017 FCC vote mattered because it showed that the legal status of broadband access in the United States was not a settled technical question, but a contested question of political power, market structure, and public-interest regulation. The decision did not "end the internet", but it did reverse a major federal regulatory approach and reopen a long-running fight over how internet service providers should be governed.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2017-2022)

Within five years, the repeal had already become one of the defining U.S. internet-policy decisions of its era. It triggered court challenges, state-level responses, and continued federal debate over whether broadband should be treated primarily as an information service or as infrastructure deserving stronger common-carrier style obligations.

### By the Mid-2020s

By the mid-2020s, the 2017 vote remained historically important less because of one immediate visible user outcome and more because it crystallised a deeper governance struggle. Its legacy endured in arguments over platform power, broadband competition, federal and state authority, transparency obligations, and the role of public regulation in communications networks that had become basic civic and economic infrastructure.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. The 2017 repeal is likely to remain a standard reference point in the modern history of U.S. internet regulation and net neutrality law.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Even if later broadband regulation evolves well beyond the frameworks debated in the 2010s, this vote will probably still matter as a clear example of how communications governance can swing sharply with political control.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. The repeal may endure as one of the episodes through which societies learned that digital infrastructure policy is not merely technical administration, but a recurring struggle over power, access, and public accountability.
