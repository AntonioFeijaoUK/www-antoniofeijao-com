## Implications

The Dyn attack mattered because it made internet dependency visible to ordinary users at scale. Many people experienced the event as if major websites had gone down independently, but the deeper lesson was structural: when a shared infrastructure provider fails under attack, large parts of the visible internet can fail with it. The incident also exposed the security weakness of poorly protected IoT devices, which had become raw material for large botnets.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2016-2021)

Within five years, the Dyn attack had already become one of the standard examples of how DDoS activity could exploit infrastructure concentration. It pushed organisations to think harder about DNS resilience, multi-provider strategies, third-party dependency risk, and the security consequences of insecure internet-connected devices deployed at enormous scale.

### By the Mid-2020s

By the mid-2020s, the Dyn incident remained historically important as a public demonstration that internet fragility does not always come from a direct attack on end-user platforms. Sometimes the weakest point is a critical service underneath them. Its legacy endured in resilience planning, incident communication, DDoS defence, and IoT security discussions.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. The Dyn attack is likely to remain one of the clearest historical examples of how dependency on shared digital infrastructure can turn a single attack into a broad internet outage.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Even if network architecture becomes more resilient, the Dyn incident will probably still matter as an early large-scale warning about platform dependency, weak IoT security, and systemic internet risk.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. The 2016 Dyn attack may endure as one of the moments when societies learned more clearly that digital infrastructure is not merely convenient plumbing, but a critical layer whose failure can ripple across communication, commerce, and daily life.
