## Implications

NotPetya mattered because it blurred several categories of cyber risk at once: ransomware, destructive malware, supply-chain compromise, and state-linked strategic disruption. It made clear that malware can be disguised in one form while functioning as something more destructive underneath. It also showed that a compromise aimed initially at one national context can cause immense collateral damage across global business networks.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2017-2022)

Within five years, NotPetya had become one of the clearest examples of cyberattack causing massive economic disruption across borders. It was remembered not as ordinary ransomware, but as a destructive operation whose effects spread through multinational firms, logistics, healthcare, and manufacturing. This forced many organisations to take software supply-chain risk and lateral movement inside networks more seriously.

### By the Mid-2020s

By the mid-2020s, NotPetya remained historically important because it demonstrated how quickly a targeted compromise could escalate into a global business crisis. Its legacy endured in incident recovery planning, network segmentation, backup strategy, software update trust, and the broader understanding that some cyber incidents are best understood as strategic disruption rather than ordinary criminal extortion.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. NotPetya is likely to remain one of the key case studies in destructive malware, software supply-chain compromise, and systemic international cyber risk.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Even if future attacks operate very differently, NotPetya will probably still be remembered as an early and especially clear example of how digital operations can cause enormous cross-border economic harm.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. NotPetya may endure as one of the major historical markers of cyber conflict in the early twenty-first century, because it showed how a software compromise could propagate far beyond its initial theatre and damage global commercial infrastructure.
