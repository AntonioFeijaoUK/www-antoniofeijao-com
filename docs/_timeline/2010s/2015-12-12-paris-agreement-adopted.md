## Implications

The adoption of the Paris Agreement mattered because it created the most important shared diplomatic framework yet for global climate action. It did not solve climate change, and its reliance on nationally determined commitments left major questions about enforcement and adequacy. Even so, it became historically decisive because it brought nearly all states into a common structure organised around long-term temperature goals, repeated review cycles, and rising expectations of national climate planning. Paris therefore marked both a diplomatic achievement and a new standard against which future climate action would be judged.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2015-2020)

Within five years, the Paris Agreement had become the central reference point for international climate diplomacy. It entered into force in 2016, shaped national pledges and reporting expectations, and established the language through which governments, businesses, and civil society increasingly discussed net zero, ambition, and the closing gap between promises and emissions reality.

### Within 10 Years (2015-2025)

Within a decade, Paris had proved historically important not because it ended dispute, but because it reorganised it. Arguments over climate responsibility, finance, adaptation, fossil fuels, and implementation were increasingly conducted inside a framework that Paris had helped formalise. The agreement also became the benchmark against which later milestones, including COP26 and subsequent stocktake processes, were evaluated.

### Within 10+ Years (2015-2025 and beyond)

Even within its first decade, the Paris Agreement had already become one of the defining documents of twenty-first-century global governance. Its legacy lies in creating a durable diplomatic architecture for a planetary problem, even while the world continued to struggle over pace, fairness, and execution. It matters historically because it made climate coordination more universal, more regular, and more measurable than earlier frameworks had managed.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. The Paris Agreement is likely to remain one of the principal benchmark documents in the history of international climate diplomacy.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Future assessments may treat Paris either as the framework that helped organise meaningful long-term mitigation or as an ambitious but insufficient attempt to govern an escalating planetary crisis.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. The Paris Agreement may endure as one of the defining governance milestones of the era in which climate change became a central organising problem of world politics.
