## Implications

The adoption of the Glasgow Climate Pact mattered because it became the clearest formal outcome of COP26 and one of the most closely scrutinised moments in post-Paris climate diplomacy. The pact did not solve the climate crisis, and many observers regarded it as insufficient. Even so, it mattered historically because it concentrated global attention on the narrowing path to keep warming closer to 1.5C, formalised further negotiation and reporting pressures, and revealed both the possibilities and limits of consensus-based climate governance.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 2 Years (2021-2023)

Within two years, the Glasgow outcome had become a reference point for debates about fossil fuels, national pledges, climate finance, adaptation, and the gap between diplomatic language and real emissions trajectories. It was especially notable because it placed unusually direct attention on coal and inefficient fossil-fuel subsidies, even though the final wording reflected compromise and dilution under political pressure.

### Within 5 Years (2021-2026)

Within five years, COP26 had come to be seen as one of the key climate summits of the early twenty-first century, not because it fully resolved the central problem, but because it exposed how little room for delay remained. The pact sharpened the contrast between scientific urgency and political bargaining, making Glasgow a durable benchmark for judging whether later conferences produced real acceleration or continued incrementalism.

### Within 10+ Years (2021-2031 and beyond)

Even within the first decade after COP26, the Glasgow Climate Pact had already become part of the historical record of how states tried to govern a planetary emergency through negotiation, compromise, and contested ambition. Its legacy lies both in what it advanced and in what it failed to deliver, making it a useful lens for understanding the strengths and weaknesses of global climate diplomacy.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. COP26 and the Glasgow Climate Pact are likely to remain a major benchmark in the history of international climate negotiations, especially in relation to the 1.5C target and fossil-fuel politics.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Future assessments may treat Glasgow either as a turning point that helped build later action or as a stark example of how slowly institutions responded to an escalating climate threat.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. The Glasgow Climate Pact may endure as one of the emblematic documents of the era in which humanity knew the scale of the climate crisis but still struggled to align political systems with scientific warnings.
