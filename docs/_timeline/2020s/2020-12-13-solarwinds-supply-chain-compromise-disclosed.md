## Implications

The SolarWinds compromise mattered because it revealed a particularly serious form of trust failure. Attackers did not need to compromise every target individually in the first instance. By corrupting a trusted software update channel, they used a routine mechanism of maintenance and trust as a delivery path. That made the event historically important not only for espionage, but for the governance of software build systems, update integrity, and dependency trust.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2020-2025)

Within five years, SolarWinds had become one of the defining supply-chain incidents of the modern era. It accelerated investment in secure software development, build-environment hardening, provenance controls, incident response maturity, and the broader recognition that software trust chains can become strategic attack surfaces.

### By the Mid-2020s

By the mid-2020s, SolarWinds remained historically important because it changed the language of software security. The incident helped push supply-chain compromise, secure build pipelines, code-signing assurance, and software bill of materials conversations into mainstream policy and enterprise security planning rather than leaving them as specialist concerns.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. SolarWinds is likely to remain one of the core case studies in software supply-chain compromise and trusted-update abuse.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Even if software distribution changes significantly, this event will probably still matter as an early and especially consequential warning that trust in updates must itself be engineered and continuously defended.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. The SolarWinds compromise may endure as one of the incidents that taught digital societies that modern infrastructure can be undermined not only by breaking trust, but by impersonating it through the channels systems already rely on every day.
