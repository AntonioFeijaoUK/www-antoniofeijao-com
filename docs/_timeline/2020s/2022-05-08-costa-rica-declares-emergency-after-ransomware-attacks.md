## Implications

The Costa Rica crisis mattered because it showed that ransomware can become a national governance problem rather than only an organisational IT emergency. When multiple state institutions are hit, the damage extends beyond files and systems into tax collection, customs, healthcare, administration, and public confidence. The emergency declaration made that escalation unmistakable.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2022-2027)

Within five years, Costa Rica's response had already become a significant case study in public-sector cyber resilience. The event underscored the vulnerability of government systems, the strain ransomware can place on national administration, and the need for cyber capacity that includes not only incident response but continuity of state operations.

### By the Mid-2020s

By the mid-2020s, the emergency remained historically important because it gave one of the clearest public demonstrations that cyberattacks can rise to the level of national emergency governance. Its legacy endured in regional cyber policy, public-sector preparedness, and international discussions about what kinds of cyber disruption should be treated as national-security and civil-protection events.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. Costa Rica's 2022 emergency is likely to remain an important reference point for how ransomware can pressure the functioning of a state, not merely a company.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. If governments continue digitising essential services without equal investment in resilience, this event will probably still matter as an early warning of how public administration can be disrupted at national scale.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. Costa Rica's ransomware emergency may endure as one of the moments when states learned more explicitly that cyber resilience is part of basic national continuity.
