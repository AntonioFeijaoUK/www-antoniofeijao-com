## Implications

The beginning of ISS assembly mattered because it transformed the idea of a permanently occupied multinational orbital laboratory into a physical construction project in space. The launch of Zarya was not the completion of the station, but it was the point at which an international partnership in human spaceflight became an operational structure in orbit. That made it a milestone in engineering, diplomacy, and long-duration mission planning.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (1998-2003)

Within five years of the first module launch, the station had received its first crews and had become a growing orbital outpost rather than a conceptual programme. Its early years established the practical reality of assembling a large inhabited structure in space out of modules launched at different times by different partners.

### Within 10 Years (1998-2008)

Within a decade, the ISS had become one of the central platforms of human spaceflight. Its significance extended into international cooperation, microgravity science, life-support operations, logistics, robotics, and the steady development of long-duration crew experience in low Earth orbit.

### Within 25+ Years (1998-2023 and beyond)

More than twenty-five years later, the launch of Zarya remained historically important as the opening step in the assembly of the largest and most durable human outpost yet built in space. Its legacy endured in research, diplomacy, engineering integration, and the demonstration that rival nations could build and operate a shared orbital laboratory across decades.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. The first ISS module launch is likely to remain the clear opening milestone of the station's construction era and of long-term multinational orbital cooperation.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Even if the ISS itself is eventually retired, the beginning of its assembly will probably remain historically important because it marked the successful start of a shared orbital infrastructure project on a scale never previously achieved.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. The start of ISS assembly may endure as one of the key milestones in the history of human presence in orbit, because it marked the point where international space cooperation became a lived engineering reality rather than a strategic ambition alone.
