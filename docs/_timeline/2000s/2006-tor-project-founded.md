## Implications

The founding of the Tor Project mattered because it gave long-term organisational form to a privacy technology that might otherwise have remained a research effort or a niche tool. Tor was important technically, but the creation of a dedicated nonprofit also mattered institutionally: it meant anonymity software could be maintained, explained, distributed, defended, and improved by a mission-driven organisation focused on internet freedom and privacy.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2006-2011)

Within five years of the Tor Project's founding, Tor had become far more visible among privacy-conscious users, activists, technologists, and people seeking to bypass censorship. The organisation helped make the tool more durable and more accessible than a purely research-led effort would likely have been.

### Within 10 Years (2006-2016)

Within a decade, Tor had become one of the most recognised names in online anonymity and censorship resistance. It mattered not only to specialists, but also to journalists, dissidents, whistleblowers, and digital-rights advocates. The founding of the Tor Project helped turn anonymity tooling into a maintained civic technology rather than an academic curiosity.

### Within 15+ Years (2006-2021 and beyond)

More than fifteen years later, the Tor Project remained historically important as one of the principal institutions behind privacy-preserving internet access. Its legacy extended into debates about surveillance, freedom of expression, law enforcement, human rights, and the technical possibility of resisting pervasive tracking online.
