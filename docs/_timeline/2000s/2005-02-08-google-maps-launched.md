## Implications

Google Maps mattered because it helped make digital maps interactive, searchable, and broadly accessible through an ordinary web browser. Instead of consulting static maps or specialised software, users could pan, zoom, search, and plan routes in a fluid online interface. That changed how people navigated cities, understood places, searched for local businesses, and expected geographic information to behave on the web.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2005-2010)

Within five years of launch, Google Maps had become one of the most recognisable internet services in everyday life. It helped normalise web-based navigation, local search, route planning, satellite imagery, and the expectation that maps should be interactive rather than static.

### Within 10 Years (2005-2015)

Within a decade, Google Maps had become one of the core infrastructures of digital navigation. Its significance extended into smartphones, logistics, local discovery, travel, urban mobility, and the broader assumption that geographic information should be instantly searchable and context-aware.

### Within 20+ Years (2005-2025 and beyond)

More than twenty years later, Google Maps remained historically important as one of the internet services that changed how people relate to physical space through digital systems. Its legacy extended into location-based services, ride-hailing, delivery logistics, local commerce, and the wider expectation that the world should be computationally searchable.
