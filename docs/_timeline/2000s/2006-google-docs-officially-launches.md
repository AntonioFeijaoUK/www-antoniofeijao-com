## Implications

Google Docs mattered because it helped move document creation and collaboration from local files and desktop applications into the browser. That changed expectations around writing, sharing, comments, revision history, and multi-user editing. Instead of treating documents as files emailed back and forth between individuals, Docs helped make them shared online workspaces that could be edited, discussed, and maintained collaboratively.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (2006-2011)

Within five years of its official launch, Google Docs had become one of the best-known examples of browser-based productivity software. It helped familiarise users with cloud-hosted documents, collaborative editing, and the idea that office work could happen online rather than being tied to one device or one locally installed application.

### Within 10 Years (2006-2016)

Within a decade, Google Docs had become one of the most influential tools in collaborative digital work. Its significance extended into schools, startups, distributed teams, journalism, administration, and everyday shared writing. It helped normalise live co-editing and browser-based productivity as ordinary expectations rather than experiments.

### Within 15+ Years (2006-2021 and beyond)

More than fifteen years later, Google Docs remained historically important as one of the services that helped define cloud-native collaboration. Its legacy extended into remote work, shared authoring, document transparency, and the broader rethinking of productivity software as a networked service rather than a boxed application.

Likelihood: high. Google Docs is likely to remain one of the clearest historical milestones in the shift from file-based office software to browser-based collaborative work.
