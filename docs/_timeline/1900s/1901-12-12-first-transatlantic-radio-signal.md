## Implications

The first transatlantic radio signal, as it is widely regarded in the standard historical account, showed that wireless communication did not have to remain local, coastal, or tied to visible range. It changed long-distance communication by demonstrating that messages could cross an ocean without relying on a physical cable, which greatly expanded what engineers, states, navies, and later commercial systems thought radio could achieve.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (1901-1906)

Within five years of Marconi's 1901 demonstration, wireless telegraphy had become a serious engineering and commercial field rather than an experimental curiosity. Governments, shipping interests, and private companies all understood that radio could become strategically important for long-distance communication, especially where cables were expensive, vulnerable, or impossible to lay.

### Within 10 Years (1901-1911)

Within a decade, radio communication had spread further into maritime use, military interest, and international communications infrastructure. The transatlantic result mattered because it helped convince the world that wireless was not merely a short-range novelty, but a practical system with global potential.

### Within 50+ Years (1901-1951 and beyond)

More than fifty years later, radio had transformed shipping, broadcasting, military coordination, emergency signalling, and the wider communications industry. The 1901 signal mattered because it marked one of the clearest early moments when global communication stopped depending entirely on wires.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. The first transatlantic radio signal is likely to remain a standard historical reference for the moment wireless communication first proved that it could operate at intercontinental scale.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. As communication systems continue shifting across radio, satellite, and networked digital layers, the 1901 signal will probably still be remembered as one of the decisive early breaks from cable-bound long-distance communication.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years
