## Implications

The standardisation of SQL mattered because it helped separate the relational-database idea from any single vendor implementation. Once SQL became a recognised standard, organisations could build applications, staff skills, and data practices around a language that was broader than one product line. That reduced lock-in, improved portability in relative terms, and made relational data systems easier to teach, adopt, and sustain across industries.

## Recorded Legacy

The sections below describe outcomes that are already part of the historical record and supported by the cited sources.

### Within 5 Years (1986-1991)

Within five years of ANSI standardisation, SQL had become more firmly established as the common language of relational database work. Vendors still developed their own dialects and extensions, but the existence of a shared standard made it easier for databases, tools, documentation, and professional training to grow around a recognisable core.

### Within 10 Years (1986-1996)

Within a decade, SQL had become one of the defining languages of business computing. It was central to the rise of large relational database systems in finance, government, enterprise software, and data management. Its importance came not only from technical capability, but from the stability that standardisation gave to long-lived institutional systems.

### Within 35+ Years (1986-2021 and beyond)

More than thirty-five years later, SQL remained one of the foundational languages of data work. Even as database systems diversified, SQL continued to shape how people queried, modelled, and thought about structured information. Its standardised core helped make it one of the most durable technical languages in modern computing.

## Forward Outlook

The sections below are projections rather than established history. They describe plausible future significance based on the event's recorded legacy.

### Possible Impact in 10 Years (to 2036)

Likelihood: high. SQL is likely to remain one of the clearest examples of how standardisation can help a technical language outlast many generations of products and platforms.

### Possible Impact in 50 Years (to 2076)

Likelihood: medium. Even if future data systems rely on additional models and abstractions, SQL will probably remain historically important because it gave structured data management a widely shared practical language.

### Possible Impact in 100+ Years

Likelihood: medium. SQL becoming an ANSI standard may endure as one of the quiet but decisive milestones in software history, because it helped make data handling more portable, teachable, and institutionally durable.
